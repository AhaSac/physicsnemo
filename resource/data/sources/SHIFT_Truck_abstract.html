Session: 12-01: AI/ML for Next-Generation Vehicle Systems

Paper Number: 197479

197479 - Introducing Shift-Truck: A Physics-Ai Foundation Model for Pickup-Truck Aerodynamics, Bootstrapped by Transfer Learning From Shift-Suv 

Abstract: 

Aerodynamic design iteration on road vehicles is bottlenecked by high-fidelity CFD cost: a single SA-DDES transient run consumes hours of GPU time, and design exploration demands hundreds of variants. Luminary's physics-AI foundation model SHIFT-SUV — co-developed with Honda and NVIDIA from approximately 4,000 DDES simulations of parametrically morphed AeroSUV variants (Luminary Cloud, 2025; HuggingFace dataset luminary-shift/SUV, CC-BY-NC-4.0) — addresses this by providing near-instant surface pressure and force inference directly from an STL geometry. In its best published configuration (GeoTransolver with Geometry-Aware Latent Embeddings, Adams et al., arXiv:2512.20399), SHIFT-SUV achieves 0.56% relative L1 error on surface pressure, 3.8% on wall shear stress, and R²(Cd) = 0.98–0.99 on Estate and Fastback holdout geometries, outperforming DoMINO, Transolver, and AB-UPT baselines. However, SHIFT-SUV is scoped to closed-body SUVs, leaving a critical gap: pickup trucks — the best-selling vehicle segment in North America — introduce open beds, sharper rear separations, taller frontal areas, optional tonneau covers, and approximately 2.5× higher Reynolds numbers, placing them well outside the SUV training distribution. This work introduces SHIFT-Truck, the second aerodynamics model in Luminary's SHIFT foundation-model family, developed by transfer learning from the SHIFT-SUV GeoTransolver checkpoint rather than training from scratch, demonstrating that a SHIFT-class physics-AI foundation model can be systematically amortized across vehicle classes.

SHIFT-Truck is trained on a target dataset of 5,000 high-fidelity SA-DDES simulations generated in Luminary's platform over a GTU-style reference pickup morphed via Blender shape-key cages, spanning 15 continuous shape parameters — cab geometry, bed dimensions, hood profile, ride height, windshield rake, chin-spoiler depth, and more — plus 2 discrete topology switches for bed cover and chin spoiler. All runs use a freestream velocity of 38.89 m/s (Re ≈ 1.4 × 10⁷) with a 5-belt rolling road and MRF wheel rotation, with solver settings intentionally bit-identical to the SHIFT-SUV pipeline to isolate cross-vehicle-class transfer as the primary variable under study. The CFD configuration was pre-validated against public automotive benchmarks — GTU/AutoCFD5, DrivAer, AeroSUV, Windsor, and the SAE notchback — at multiple mesh resolutions, locking in the SA-DDES + FDS χ 0.5/0.95 + Van Albada face-limiter (HYBRID low-dissipation) setup before production runs, ensuring accuracy differences between SHIFT-SUV and SHIFT-Truck are attributable to the transfer strategy rather than CFD inconsistencies. Five fine-tuning strategies are benchmarked — from-scratch baseline, full fine-tune, frozen-backbone head retrain, progressive unfreeze, and low-LR fine-tune — alongside a 35–350-case data-efficiency sweep mapping precisely where SUV pretraining stops paying off. The presentation will report surface pressure L1, drag and lift R², and the SUV→Truck data-efficiency curve. SHIFT-Truck is, to our knowledge, the first physics-AI foundation model for pickup-truck aerodynamics, and demonstrates a composable approach to foundation model development across vehicle platforms — shortening design iteration from days to minutes and supporting more aggressive drag-reduction targets across both ICE and electrified light-duty powertrains.

Presenting Author: Yin Yu Luminary AI

Presenting Author Biography: Yin Yu is a Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer at Luminary, working at the intersection of simulation and scientific ML. His work focuses on how modeling, data, and physics come together in practical engineering settings, spanning CAE and CFD workflows where he designs data pipelines, builds surrogate models, and develops the tooling needed to validate and deploy them. Yin holds a Ph.D. in Aerospace Engineering from Penn State, where his earlier research was in time-series prediction on graph-structured systems. He now applies similar ideas to simulation data and scientific modeling, focusing on developing reliable, end-to-end solutions that make engineering and scientific analyses faster and more scalable.
